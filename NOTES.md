# Notes — Midnight Fridge Roast

## The entry

> My fridge locks itself at midnight and flashes 'self-control mode' — a diet plan written by an appliance that has never tasted a single thing.

142 characters, one line. The program writes exactly this line to
`/output/entry.txt`, no trailing newline.

## Why this line

- **Criterion 1 (a roast of a smart fridge that refuses to open after
  midnight):** the midnight lock is the whole subject, not a side detail.
- **Criterion 2 (a real joke with a turn):** the setup is the fridge's
  "self-control mode"; the punchline is that the diet plan comes from an
  appliance that has never tasted a single thing — the fridge has no
  standing to lecture anyone about food.
- **Criterion 3 (tight):** one sentence, one joke, no second gag crowding
  the first.
- **Criterion 4 (the midnight refusal is the target):** the laugh comes
  from the fridge's petty rule itself, not from generic smart-home
  grumbling.
- **Criterion 5 (sounds spoken aloud):** a single flowing sentence someone
  could actually say standing at a locked fridge door at 12:05am.
- **Fresh angle:** Sarip's earlier fridge entries used the 3am visit loop
  ("I contain leftovers, not answers"), the door-open/electric-bill log,
  and the ice-maker slot machine. This one uses none of those — the
  fridge as an unqualified diet coach.

## Revision made after the first run

First draft opened "After midnight my fridge locks itself and flashes
'self-control mode' — ...". The revision reorders the opening to "My
fridge locks itself at midnight and flashes 'self-control mode' — ...":
the subject comes first, which reads more like something a person
actually says out loud standing at a locked fridge door (criterion 5),
and it drops three words, tightening the line from 145 to 142
characters. The punchline ("a diet plan written by an appliance that
has never tasted a single thing") is unchanged — it was the strongest
part of the draft.

## Alternatives considered and discarded

1. "My smart fridge locks at midnight and tells me it's past my bedtime
   — I didn't buy an appliance, I adopted a strict parent."
   Discarded: the "strict parent" turn is decent, but "past my bedtime"
   makes the joke about bedtime, not about the fridge's rule — the
   midnight refusal itself is not the target.
2. "After midnight my fridge turns into a bouncer — no entry, no list,
   and it already decided I'm not getting in."
   Discarded: the bouncer image is a familiar gag, and "no list" is a
   second joke crowding the first; the line also never says *why* the
   fridge refuses, so the petty rule stays vague.
3. "My fridge has a curfew now — after midnight it locks itself and
   lectures me about self-control, like a fridge that read one
   self-help book."
   Discarded: "read one self-help book" is a soft ending with no
   surprise; the chosen line's "never tasted a single thing" lands
   harder and is fridge-specific.
4. "At midnight my fridge turns back into a pumpkin — except the pumpkin
   is locked and judging my life choices."
   Discarded: the Cinderella reference is a well-worn joke, and "judging
   my life choices" is generic — it could be said about any appliance.
5. "My fridge locks at midnight to 'help my health' — a diet plan from
   an appliance that has never tasted anything."
   Discarded: an earlier, flatter draft of the chosen line; "flashes
   'self-control mode'" is a concrete, visible detail that makes the
   fridge's petty rule real, and "a single thing" is a tighter ending
   than "anything".
6. "You left my door open for 47 seconds..." — not an alternative of
   mine; it is Sarip's earlier passive-aggressive fridge entry
   (door-open / electric bill / logs). Listed here only to record that
   the angle is already used and was deliberately avoided.
